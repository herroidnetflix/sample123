GUIDE 3

Creator tips and FAQ

A short routine you can follow before every upload.

Before you post

  1. Is every clip, song, image and font yours or licensed?
  2. Do you have written permission or a licence saved?
  3. Does the licence allow your use, such as commercial or edits?
  4. Have you kept your raw files and project files as proof?
  5. If unsure, do not post, or ask the owner first.

Do

  • Use your own footage and music
  • Use licensed or royalty-free libraries
  • Read licence terms fully
  • Keep receipts and permission emails
  • Remove disputed content promptly

Don't

  • Rely on "no copyright intended"
  • Upload film scenes or songs without permission
  • Re-upload after a takedown
  • Link to or host pirated content
  • File false claims

FAQ

Are movie clips and edits legal to post?

Usually not without permission. Fair dealing covers limited purposes such as criticism or review, and a court decides case by case. A licence from the owner is the safest route.

Is a claim the same as a strike?

No. A claim lets the owner monetise or block a video. A strike is a formal penalty that can add up to channel termination.

Does DMCA apply if I live in India?

If your content is on a US platform, its DMCA process applies. Indian law, including the Copyright Act, 1957, can apply too.

What if someone copies my work?

Save your originals and dates, then use the platform's copyright report form. For serious cases speak to an advocate.

What is Creative Commons?

A set of licences that let creators grant reuse permissions with conditions. Always read the exact licence before using the work.

Can I use music from a free library?

Yes, if the library's licence covers your platform and use. Keep the licence text or a screenshot.

Note: This site shares general information to help creators understand copyright. It is not legal advice. For a specific problem, speak to a qualified advocate.
