GUIDE 1

Copyright basics for creators

Copyright is the legal right of a creator to control how an original work is copied, shared and adapted.

What copyright protects

Copyright protects original expression: films, scenes, songs (music, lyrics and recordings), photos, videos, artwork, writing and software. It protects the way an idea is expressed, not the idea itself, and it does not cover plain facts.

In India it arises automatically when the work is created, under the Copyright Act, 1957. Registration is optional, though it can help as evidence in a dispute.

Who owns it

A film is usually controlled by its producer or studio. A song often has several owners: the composer and lyricist for the composition, and a label or producer for the sound recording. Owning a copy, such as a ticket, a DVD or a download, does not give you the copyright.

How long it lasts

  • Books, music compositions and art: generally the creator's life plus 60 years.
  • Films and sound recordings: generally 60 years from the start of the year after publication.

Terms can differ by work type, so check the Act or the Copyright Office for a specific case.

What owners control

Owners can decide who may copy, publish, perform, adapt, translate or share the work online. Creators also have moral rights, including the right to be credited and to object to distortion that harms their reputation.

Fair dealing in India

Section 52 lists limited exceptions, such as private study and research, criticism or review, and reporting current events. It is narrower than the US idea of fair use. There is no fixed percentage or number of seconds. Courts usually look at the purpose, how much was used and whether it hurts the owner's market.

Licences and public domain

You may reuse a work with the owner's permission or under a licence. Creative Commons licences let creators grant permissions with conditions such as credit or non-commercial use. Works whose copyright has expired are in the public domain, but a new recording or edition of an old work may still be protected.

Note: This site shares general information to help creators understand copyright. It is not legal advice. For a specific problem, speak to a qualified advocate.
